Google account sync vs local network sync

  • Chrome Sync (Google account) Requires a Google account. Tab data is uploaded to Google servers. Works across the internet. Tied to one browser ecosystem. Google can read and process your tab URLs.
  • TabSyncBridge (local network) No account needed. Tab data stays on your own Wi-Fi. Works across Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, and Vivaldi. No external server. No analytics or tracking.

What you keep private

Your tab URLs reveal what you read, research, shop for, and work on. When you use Chrome Sync, that data is uploaded to Google's servers and associated with your Google account. TabSyncBridge keeps it between your own devices:

  • No tab data leaves your network Tab titles and URLs travel directly between your iPhone and desktop over your private Wi-Fi.
  • No tracking or analytics TabSyncBridge includes no third-party analytics SDKs, advertising SDKs, or tracking pixels.
  • No developer relay server There is no external server between your devices. The sync happens peer-to-peer on your local network.
  • No cloud account of any kind No Google account, no Apple ID for sync (only for purchase), no TabSyncBridge account. Just your own Wi-Fi.

Works with every Chromium browser

TabSyncBridge is not limited to Chrome. The same extension and desktop bridge work with Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and any other Chromium-based browser. You can even sync tabs between different Chromium browsers on the same machine through your iPhone.

Getting started

Install the iPhone app to start a free 3-month trial. Add the free browser extension and follow the desktop bridge setup guide. Connect both devices to the same Wi-Fi and you are ready to go.
